# Surveillance & Privacy Glossary

A plain-language glossary of surveillance, privacy, cybersecurity, policy, and research terms used throughout the Community Cyber Defense Project.

This glossary is designed to help:

- Residents
- Students
- Nonprofits
- Community organizations
- Journalists
- Researchers
- Public officials
- Cybersecurity professionals

understand common terms without requiring a technical background.

---

# How to Use This Glossary

Each term includes a short explanation.

Some terms may have different meanings depending on:

- Technology
- Law
- Agency policy
- Vendor documentation
- Technical context

Where precision matters, users should review the original source or applicable policy.

---

# A

## Access Control

A security process that determines who is allowed to access a system, account, file, or type of data.

Access controls can limit:

- Who can sign in
- What information a user can view
- What actions a user can perform
- Whether a user can change settings
- Whether a user can export information

---

## Access Log

A record showing when a user or system accessed information.

An access log may record:

- User
- Date
- Time
- Resource accessed
- Action performed

---

## Account Lifecycle

The process of creating, managing, reviewing, and removing user accounts.

A strong account lifecycle may include:

- Approval before access
- Role assignment
- Periodic review
- Access changes
- Account removal when no longer needed

---

## Aggregated Data

Information that has been combined into groups or summaries.

For example:

> Total number of system searches during a month.

Aggregated data may reduce some privacy risks, but aggregation does not automatically make information anonymous.

---

## Algorithm

A defined set of instructions used by a computer system to process information or produce an output.

Algorithms may be used for:

- Matching
- Classification
- Ranking
- Detection
- Prediction
- Search

---

## ALPR

**Automated License Plate Reader**

A camera and software system designed to detect and record license plates.

An ALPR record may contain:

- License plate text
- Image
- Date
- Time
- Camera location
- Vehicle characteristics

Specific capabilities vary by system and configuration.

---

## Amendment

A formal change to an existing contract, policy, ordinance, or agreement.

An amendment may change:

- Price
- Contract length
- Device count
- Services
- Retention
- Terms
- Features

---

## API

**Application Programming Interface**

A method that allows different software systems to communicate with each other.

An API may allow one system to:

- Request information
- Send information
- Search another service
- Automate processes

APIs can increase interoperability but may also increase data-sharing and security considerations.

---

## Audit

A structured review used to determine whether activity follows defined rules, policies, contracts, or controls.

An audit may review:

- Searches
- User access
- Data sharing
- Retention
- Security
- Misuse
- Policy compliance

---

## Audit Log

A record of activity occurring inside a system.

Audit logs may record:

- Logins
- Searches
- Data access
- Downloads
- Administrative changes
- Sharing changes
- Account changes

Audit logs can support accountability and security investigations.

---

## Authentication

The process of verifying that a user is who they claim to be.

Common authentication methods include:

- Password
- Passkey
- Security key
- Authentication app
- Biometric factor

---

## Authorization

The process of determining what an authenticated user is allowed to do.

Authentication asks:

> Who are you?

Authorization asks:

> What are you allowed to access?

---

# B

## Biometric Data

Information based on biological or physical characteristics.

Examples may include:

- Face geometry
- Fingerprints
- Iris patterns
- Voice characteristics

Biometric data can raise significant privacy and security concerns because biological characteristics may be difficult or impossible to replace if compromised.

---

## Biometric Identification

A process that attempts to determine a person's identity by comparing biometric information against multiple stored identities.

Example:

> Comparing a face image against a database of known faces.

---

## Biometric Verification

A process that compares biometric information against a claimed identity.

Example:

> Using Face ID to verify the person attempting to unlock a phone.

---

## Breach

A security event involving unauthorized access to or disclosure of information.

The exact legal or contractual definition of a breach may vary.

---

## Breach Notification

A requirement or process for notifying affected organizations or individuals after certain security incidents.

---

# C

## Camera Network

A collection of cameras connected through a common platform, network, or monitoring system.

A network may allow:

- Centralized viewing
- Search
- Analytics
- Remote access
- Shared access

---

## Case Number

A unique identifier assigned to an investigation, incident, or matter.

Some surveillance systems may require a user to enter a case number before conducting certain searches.

---

## Cell-Site Simulator

A device that imitates a cellular tower in order to interact with nearby mobile devices.

These systems may be used by law enforcement under specific legal and operational circumstances.

They are sometimes known by product names such as Stingray.

---

## Cloud Computing

The use of remote computing infrastructure and services delivered over a network.

Cloud services may provide:

- Storage
- Databases
- Applications
- Processing
- Backups

---

## Cloud Hosting

When a system or its data is stored or operated using a cloud service provider rather than only on local government equipment.

---

## Community Oversight

Processes that allow residents, elected officials, civilian bodies, or other outside groups to review government programs.

Examples:

- Public hearings
- Annual reports
- Civilian oversight boards
- Public approval
- Published audits

---

## Contract

A legally binding agreement between parties.

For surveillance technology, contracts may address:

- Cost
- Services
- Data
- Retention
- Security
- Access
- Renewal
- Termination

---

## Contract Renewal

The continuation of a contract beyond its current term.

Renewal may be:

- Automatic
- Optional
- Negotiated
- Subject to approval

---

## Correlation

A relationship between two events or variables.

Correlation alone does not prove that one caused the other.

---

## Cybersecurity

The practice of protecting systems, networks, devices, accounts, and information from unauthorized access, misuse, disruption, or attack.

---

# D

## Data Broker

A company that collects, combines, analyzes, or sells information about individuals.

Data may come from:

- Public records
- Apps
- Websites
- Purchases
- Location data
- Advertising networks
- Other commercial sources

---

## Data Collection

The process of gathering information.

---

## Data Deletion

The process of removing stored information.

Questions about deletion may include:

- Is deletion automatic?
- Are backups deleted?
- Can preserved records remain?
- Is deletion verified?

---

## Data Lifecycle

The stages information moves through from creation or collection to eventual deletion.

A simplified lifecycle may include:

**Collection → Processing → Storage → Access → Sharing → Retention → Deletion**

---

## Data Minimization

The principle of collecting, using, and retaining only the information reasonably necessary for a specific purpose.

---

## Data Processing

Any operation performed on information.

Examples:

- Collecting
- Organizing
- Searching
- Matching
- Analyzing
- Sharing
- Storing
- Deleting

---

## Data Retention

The amount of time information is stored before deletion.

---

## Data Sharing

Providing information or access to another person, agency, organization, or system.

---

## Data Sharing Agreement

A formal agreement that establishes how information may be shared between organizations.

---

## Data Subject

A person whose information is being collected or processed.

---

## Data Portability

The ability to move or export information from one system to another.

---

## Data Residency

Rules or requirements concerning where information is physically or geographically stored.

---

## Database

An organized collection of information that can be stored, searched, updated, and retrieved.

---

## De-Identification

A process intended to remove or reduce information that identifies individuals.

De-identification does not always guarantee that a person cannot be identified again.

---

## Deepfake

Synthetic or manipulated media created using artificial intelligence or other digital techniques to make a person appear to say or do something they did not actually say or do.

---

## Device Identifier

A value used to identify or distinguish a device.

Examples may include:

- Hardware identifiers
- Advertising identifiers
- Network identifiers

---

## Digital Footprint

The information created or left behind through digital activity.

Examples:

- Social media activity
- Search history
- App activity
- Location information
- Online purchases

---

## Digital Surveillance

The use of digital technology to observe, collect, monitor, analyze, or track information about people, places, devices, or activities.

---

## Doxxing

Publishing private or identifying information about someone, typically without their consent and often with harmful intent.

The Community Cyber Defense Project does not support doxxing.

---

# E

## Encryption

A process that transforms readable information into a protected form that requires authorized access to read.

---

## Encryption at Rest

Protection applied to information while it is stored.

---

## Encryption in Transit

Protection applied to information while it moves between systems.

---

## Evidence

Information used to support a finding or conclusion.

Examples:

- Contract
- Policy
- Government record
- Audit log
- Official report
- Technical documentation

---

## Evidence Strength

An assessment of how strongly a source supports a finding.

The Community Cyber Defense Project may classify evidence as:

- High
- Moderate
- Low
- Unknown

---

## Evidence Log

A structured list of sources used during research.

---

# F

## Facial Recognition

Technology used to analyze facial characteristics and compare facial images.

Different systems may perform:

- Verification
- Identification
- Face detection
- Similarity comparison

---

## False Match

When a system incorrectly indicates that two records or identities match.

---

## False Negative

When a system fails to identify something that is actually present.

---

## False Positive

When a system indicates something is present when it is not.

---

## Flock Safety

A technology company known for providing automated license plate reader systems and related public-safety technologies.

Vendor documentation should be treated as vendor-provided information unless independently verified.

---

## FOIA

**Freedom of Information Act**

A United States federal law that provides public access to certain federal government records.

FOIA applies to federal agencies.

State and local records are governed by state public-records or open-records laws.

---

# G

## Geolocation

Information describing the geographic location of a person, device, vehicle, or object.

---

## Geofence

A virtual geographic boundary.

Software can perform an action when a device or event is associated with a defined geographic area.

---

## Government Transparency

The practice of making government information and decision-making more accessible to the public.

---

## Gunshot Detection

Technology designed to detect sounds that may represent gunfire and estimate where the sound originated.

---

# H

## Hash

A value produced by a mathematical function that converts information into a fixed-length output.

Hashes can help verify whether a file has changed.

---

## Hotlist

A list of identifiers that a system is configured to alert on.

For ALPR systems, a hotlist may contain license plates associated with specific investigative or public-safety purposes.

Policies should define how hotlists are created and used.

---

## Human Review

When a person reviews an automated system's result before additional action is taken.

Human review can help identify:

- Errors
- Context
- False matches
- Incorrect alerts

---

# I

## Incident Response

A structured process for responding to cybersecurity incidents.

Typical stages may include:

- Detection
- Analysis
- Containment
- Recovery
- Review

---

## Independent Audit

An audit performed by a person or organization sufficiently independent from the people who routinely operate the system being reviewed.

---

## Internet of Things

**IoT**

Physical devices connected to networks that can collect, transmit, or receive information.

Examples:

- Cameras
- Sensors
- Smart traffic systems
- Smart home devices

---

## Investigative Lead

Information that may help guide an investigation.

A lead is not automatically proof of wrongdoing.

---

# J

## Jurisdiction

The geographic area or legal authority within which a government body operates.

Examples:

- City
- County
- State
- Federal

---

# L

## Law Enforcement Database

A database used by law-enforcement agencies for operational or investigative purposes.

---

## Least Privilege

A cybersecurity principle where users receive only the access necessary to perform their responsibilities.

---

## License Plate Reader

See:

**ALPR — Automated License Plate Reader**

---

## Location Data

Information that indicates where a person, device, vehicle, or object is or has been.

---

# M

## Metadata

Information describing other information.

Examples:

- Date
- Time
- Location
- File size
- Device
- Account

Metadata can be highly revealing even when it does not include the full underlying content.

---

## MFA

**Multi-Factor Authentication**

A security method requiring more than one type of authentication factor.

Examples may include:

- Password + authentication app
- Password + security key
- Passkey + device verification

---

## Mission Creep

When a technology or program gradually expands beyond its original stated purpose.

Example:

A system introduced for one narrow use later being used for additional purposes without equivalent review.

---

## Model Policy

A sample policy framework that organizations may adapt when developing their own policy.

A model policy is not automatically law.

---

# N

## NIST

**National Institute of Standards and Technology**

A United States government agency that develops technical standards, frameworks, measurements, and cybersecurity resources.

---

## Network Sharing

When access to information or a system is extended across multiple participating organizations or agencies.

---

# O

## Open Data

Government or organizational information published in a format that can be accessed and reused by the public.

---

## Open Records Law

A law governing public access to government records.

Terminology varies by jurisdiction.

---

## Oversight

Processes used to supervise, review, audit, or evaluate government or organizational activity.

---

# P

## Personally Identifiable Information

**PII**

Information that can identify or be linked to a specific person.

Examples can include:

- Name
- Address
- Government identification number
- Email address
- Phone number

The exact definition varies by legal and organizational context.

---

## Policy

A written rule or set of rules explaining how an organization intends to operate.

---

## Policy vs. Practice

A comparison between:

> What written policy requires

and:

> What available evidence shows actually occurs.

---

## Predictive Policing

The use of data analysis or statistical systems to make predictions related to crime, locations, or enforcement activity.

The exact meaning and technology involved can vary significantly.

---

## Preservation

Keeping information beyond its normal retention period.

For example, information may be preserved because it is associated with an investigation, legal requirement, or records obligation.

---

## Privacy

The ability of individuals to have meaningful control over information about themselves and how it is collected, used, shared, and retained.

Definitions of privacy vary by legal and social context.

---

## Privacy Impact Assessment

A structured review used to identify and evaluate privacy risks associated with a program, technology, or system.

---

## Procurement

The government or organizational process for acquiring products and services.

Procurement records may include:

- Requests for proposals
- Vendor bids
- Purchase orders
- Contracts
- Evaluations
- Approval documents

---

## Public Comment

A process that allows residents to provide comments during certain government meetings or decision-making processes.

Rules vary by government body.

---

## Public Records

Records created or received by government agencies in connection with public business, subject to applicable public-records law and exemptions.

Definitions vary by jurisdiction.

---

## Public Records Request

A request for government records made under applicable public-records or open-records law.

---

# R

## RBAC

**Role-Based Access Control**

A method of assigning system permissions based on a user's organizational role.

Example:

An administrator may have different permissions than an investigator.

---

## Real-Time Crime Center

A centralized facility or operation that may combine technologies and information sources to support law-enforcement operations.

Capabilities vary significantly across jurisdictions.

---

## Records Retention Schedule

A documented schedule that specifies how long different categories of government or organizational records must be kept.

---

## Red Flag

A condition that warrants additional review.

Within this project, a red flag does not automatically mean:

- Illegal
- Abusive
- Improper
- Malicious

It means additional investigation may be appropriate.

---

## Redisclosure

When information received from one source is shared again with another party.

---

## Retention Period

The amount of time information is stored.

---

## Risk

The possibility that an event or condition could cause harm.

Privacy or cybersecurity risk may consider:

- Likelihood
- Impact
- Exposure
- Vulnerability

---

# S

## Safeguard

A technical, administrative, contractual, policy, or legal measure intended to reduce risk or improve accountability.

---

## Search Justification

A reason entered or documented by a user explaining why a database search is being conducted.

---

## Search Log

A record containing information about searches conducted in a system.

---

## Security Incident

An event that may threaten:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
- Authorized access

Not every security incident is necessarily a confirmed breach.

---

## Security Key

A physical or device-based authentication tool used to verify a user's identity.

---

## Sensitive Data

Information that may create meaningful harm, privacy risk, or security risk if exposed or misused.

---

## Sensitive Location

A location where surveillance may raise additional privacy, civil-liberties, safety, or confidentiality concerns.

Examples may include:

- Medical facilities
- Legal services
- Religious institutions
- Schools
- Domestic violence services

Context matters.

---

## Service-Level Agreement

**SLA**

A contract section or separate agreement defining service expectations.

Examples:

- Availability
- Response time
- Support
- Recovery

---

## Social Media Monitoring

The use of software or human analysis to monitor publicly available or otherwise accessible social-media activity.

Specific legal and policy rules depend on how the monitoring is conducted.

---

## SOC 2

A type of independent examination involving controls at a service organization related to categories such as security, availability, confidentiality, processing integrity, or privacy.

A SOC 2 report does not mean a system can never be compromised.

---

## Source

The document, record, interview, dataset, website, or other material used to support a research finding.

---

## Source Verification

The process of checking whether a source is authentic, current, relevant, and accurately represented.

---

## Statement of Work

**SOW**

A document describing work, services, deliverables, or requirements under a contract.

---

## Stingray

A commonly recognized name associated with certain cell-site simulator technologies.

See:

**Cell-Site Simulator**

---

## Subcontractor

A separate company or organization used by a primary vendor to provide part of a service.

---

## Subprocessor

A company that processes information on behalf of another organization or vendor.

---

## Surveillance

The observation, monitoring, collection, or analysis of information about people, places, devices, vehicles, activities, or environments.

Surveillance may be conducted by:

- Governments
- Companies
- Institutions
- Individuals

---

## Surveillance Technology

Technology capable of collecting, observing, analyzing, tracking, or monitoring information.

Examples may include:

- Cameras
- ALPRs
- Facial recognition
- Drones
- Location tracking
- Cell-site simulators
- Social-media monitoring

---

# T

## Technical Control

A safeguard implemented through technology.

Examples:

- MFA
- Encryption
- Role-based access
- Logging

---

## Third Party

A person, company, agency, or organization other than the primary parties involved in a transaction or system.

---

## Threat

Something capable of causing harm to a system, organization, person, or information.

---

## Transparency Portal

A website or online dashboard used to publish information about a government program or technology.

---

# U

## Unauthorized Access

Access to a system or information without appropriate permission.

---

## Unknown

A finding status used when reliable information has not been located.

Unknown does not mean:

- Yes
- No
- Good
- Bad
- Misconduct

It means more evidence is needed.

---

## Uptime

The amount or percentage of time a system is operational and available.

---

# V

## Vendor

A company that provides a product or service.

---

## Vendor Claim

A statement made by a vendor that has not necessarily been independently verified.

---

## Version Control

A process for recording and managing changes to files, policies, code, or reports over time.

---

## Vulnerability

A weakness that may be exploited or contribute to security risk.

---

## Vulnerability Management

The process of:

- Identifying
- Assessing
- Prioritizing
- Remediating
- Tracking

security vulnerabilities.

---

# W

## Watchlist

A list maintained for monitoring specific identifiers, events, systems, or future developments.

Within the Community Cyber Defense Project, a surveillance watchlist refers to monitoring civic developments such as:

- Contract renewals
- Procurement
- Policy changes
- New technology

It does not refer to targeting individuals.

---

## Warrant

A legal authorization issued by a court under applicable law.

Legal requirements vary depending on the type of search, technology, jurisdiction, and circumstances.

# Common ALPR Terms

## Plate Read

A record created when an ALPR system detects a license plate.

---

## Detection

A system observation of a vehicle or plate.

A detection does not automatically mean criminal activity occurred.

---

## Hotlist Alert

A notification triggered when a detected license plate matches an identifier on a configured list.

An alert should not automatically be treated as proof that:

- The driver is the registered owner
- A crime occurred
- The alert is accurate

Human verification may be necessary.

---

## Historical Search

A search of previously collected ALPR records.

---

## Network

A group of agencies or systems that can share or search information across participating organizations.

---

## Camera Location

The location where an ALPR device captured a detection.

Publishing exact locations may not always be appropriate when doing so creates legitimate security concerns.

---

# Common Facial Recognition Terms

## Face Detection

Determining whether a face appears in an image.

Detection does not necessarily identify the person.

---

## Face Template

A mathematical representation derived from facial characteristics.

---

## One-to-One Comparison

Comparing one face against one claimed identity.

Often written:

**1:1**

---

## One-to-Many Search

Comparing one face against many stored identities.

Often written:

**1:N**

---

## Candidate List

A list of possible matches produced by a facial-recognition system.

A candidate is not automatically a confirmed identity.

---

# Common Cybersecurity Terms

## Confidentiality

Protecting information from unauthorized disclosure.

---

## Integrity

Protecting information from unauthorized or improper alteration.

---

## Availability

Ensuring authorized users can access systems and information when needed.

Together:

**Confidentiality + Integrity + Availability**

are often called the:

**CIA Triad**

---

## Least Privilege

Users receive only the permissions they need.

---

## Defense in Depth

Using multiple layers of security rather than relying on one safeguard.

---

## Zero Trust

A security approach that avoids automatically trusting users or systems solely because of network location.

Access should be continuously evaluated based on identity, device, permissions, and context.

# Important Distinctions

## Policy Is Not Practice

A written policy does not automatically prove that the policy is followed.

---

## Vendor Claim Is Not Independent Verification

Vendor documentation can be valuable primary information about the vendor's product.

It should still be identified as vendor-provided information.

---

## Unknown Is Not Negative

Lack of evidence should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of wrongdoing.

---

## Alert Is Not Proof

An automated alert is an investigative signal, not necessarily confirmation.

---

## Technology Is Not Policy

A system may technically support a feature even when agency policy prohibits its use.

---

## Capability Is Not Deployment

A vendor may offer a feature that a specific customer has not enabled.

---

## Access Is Not Ownership

An organization may not own data but may still have the ability to access it.

---

## Retention Is Not Preservation

Routine retention describes how long information is normally kept.

Preservation refers to keeping specific information beyond normal retention.

---

## Security Certification Is Not Security Guarantee

Security audits and certifications can provide valuable evidence about controls.

They do not guarantee that a system cannot experience a security incident.
