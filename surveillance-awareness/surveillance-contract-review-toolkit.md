# Surveillance Contract Review Toolkit

A practical framework for reviewing contracts, agreements, procurement records, and related documents involving surveillance technology.

This toolkit is designed for:

- Nonprofits
- Community organizations
- Journalists
- Researchers
- Students
- Privacy advocates
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Public officials
- Residents conducting local surveillance research

The goal is to help users identify the provisions that matter most when reviewing government surveillance contracts.

---

# Why Contracts Matter

A surveillance policy may explain how an agency says it will use a system.

A contract may reveal additional information about:

- Cost
- Subscription terms
- Renewal
- Cancellation
- Data ownership
- Vendor access
- Data retention
- Security responsibilities
- Hosting
- Subcontractors
- Breach notification
- Product changes
- Data deletion
- Termination

Contracts should be reviewed alongside:

- Agency policy
- Procurement records
- Statements of work
- Amendments
- Vendor documentation
- Public meeting records
- Public-records responses

No single document should automatically be treated as the complete picture.

---

# Contract Review Workflow

Use the following process:

**COLLECT → ORGANIZE → IDENTIFY → EXTRACT → COMPARE → VERIFY → FLAG → REPORT → TRACK**

# Contract Red-Flag Register

Potential areas requiring additional review include:

- Automatic renewal with short cancellation window
- Missing termination rights
- Unclear data ownership
- Unknown retention
- No deletion provision
- Unclear vendor access
- Unclear subcontractors
- Weak breach-notification requirements
- No audit rights
- No clear security obligations
- Online terms vendor can change unilaterally
- No export rights
- No contract-termination data deletion
- Large cost increases
- Unclear external sharing

A red flag is:

> A reason for additional review.

It is not proof of misconduct or illegality.

---

# Positive Safeguards Register

Document protections too.

Examples:

- Strong termination rights
- Clear data ownership
- Short defined retention
- Automatic deletion
- MFA
- Role-based access
- Search logs
- Strong breach notification
- Independent security assessments
- Audit rights
- Restricted subcontractor access
- Certified deletion
- Public renewal process

# Questions for Government Officials

1. When does the contract expire?
2. What is the cancellation deadline?
3. What is the full annual cost?
4. Who owns locally collected data?
5. How long is data retained?
6. Can vendor employees access the data?
7. Which subcontractors process the data?
8. Is MFA mandatory?
9. Are searches logged?
10. What security assessments are required?
11. How quickly must the vendor report a breach?
12. What happens to the data when the contract ends?
13. Can the vendor change incorporated online terms?
14. Can the government export its data?
15. What review occurs before renewal?

---

# Questions for Vendors

1. Under what circumstances can employees access customer data?
2. Is privileged vendor access logged?
3. Which subprocessors handle customer information?
4. Is MFA required?
5. What activities are included in audit logs?
6. What is the standard retention model?
7. How is deletion verified?
8. What happens to backups after deletion?
9. What independent security assessments are performed?
10. What is the breach-notification process?
11. Can customers export audit logs?
12. What happens to customer data after termination?
13. Can product terms change during the contract?
14. Are new analytics enabled automatically?
15. Is customer data used for AI or product development?

Vendor responses should be labeled as vendor statements unless independently verified.

# Core Principle

**The contract does not tell the whole story, but it often tells you where to ask the next question.**

Review the contract, compare it with policy, verify it against practice, and document what remains unknown.
