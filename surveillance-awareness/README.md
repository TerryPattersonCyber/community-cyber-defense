# Surveillance Awareness

A practical, evidence-based resource hub for understanding, investigating, evaluating, and responding to surveillance technologies used in communities.

This section is part of the [Community Cyber Defense Project](../README.md).

The goal is not only to explain surveillance technology.

It is to give people a repeatable process for moving from:

> “I know this technology exists, but I do not understand how it works or how it is governed.”

to:

> “I can identify the technology, locate the evidence, evaluate the safeguards, understand the unknowns, and decide what action makes sense.”

# Core Research Principle

> **Document first. Evaluate second. Advocate from evidence.**

The framework separates:

- Evidence
- Government statements
- Vendor statements
- Advocacy claims
- Research analysis
- Unknown information

This helps reduce unsupported assumptions and makes published findings easier to evaluate.

---

# Finding Labels

Research throughout this project may use the following labels.

## VERIFIED

Supported by reliable primary evidence.

## REPORTED

Supported by credible secondary reporting.

## AGENCY CLAIM

Stated by a government agency but not independently verified.

## VENDOR CLAIM

Stated by a vendor.

## ADVOCACY CLAIM

Stated by an advocacy organization.

## ANALYSIS

A conclusion based on documented evidence.

## UNVERIFIED

Information exists but has not been adequately confirmed.

## UNKNOWN

Reliable information has not been located.

# Questions Every Community Can Ask

## Deployment

- What technology is being used?
- How many systems or devices are deployed?
- Who operates them?

## Purpose

- Why was the technology acquired?
- What uses are authorized?
- What uses are prohibited?

## Data

- What information is collected?
- How long is it retained?
- How is it deleted?

## Access

- Who can access the system?
- Are individual accounts required?
- Is MFA required?

## Search

- Are searches logged?
- Must users document a reason?

## Sharing

- Which organizations can access the data?
- Can information be shared outside the jurisdiction?

## Cybersecurity

- How is access protected?
- Are security incidents tracked?
- What access does the vendor have?

## Auditing

- Who reviews activity?
- How often?

## Effectiveness

- What outcomes are measured?
- How are those outcomes defined?

## Governance

- Who approved the system?
- When does the contract expire?
- What happens before renewal?

# What This Project Does Not Assume

The existence of surveillance technology does not automatically prove:

- Misconduct
- Illegal activity
- Abuse
- Effective crime reduction
- Ineffective crime reduction
- Adequate safeguards
- Inadequate safeguards

Those questions require evidence.

---

# Research Before Conclusions

When information is unavailable, use:

**UNKNOWN**

not:

> The agency must be hiding it.

When a vendor states something:

Use:

**VENDOR CLAIM**

until appropriate verification exists.

When a government agency states something:

Use:

**AGENCY CLAIM**

unless independently supported.

# Privacy by Design

The project itself should minimize unnecessary personal information.

Research should focus primarily on:

- Systems
- Policies
- Contracts
- Vendors
- Government decisions
- Public records
- Technical safeguards

rather than collecting unnecessary personal data about individuals.

Future technical versions of the project should consider:

- MFA
- Least privilege
- Secure authentication
- Encryption

- Secure storage
- Backups

- Secure development

# Responsible AI

Future AI-assisted features should:

- Cite sources
- Identify uncertainty
- Avoid inventing evidence
- Avoid automated legal conclusions
- Preserve conflicting information
- Allow human review
- Record important transformations
- Protect sensitive information

AI should assist research.

It should not replace verification.
