# Surveillance Policy Scorecard

A standardized, evidence-based framework for evaluating the privacy, cybersecurity, transparency, accountability, and community-oversight safeguards surrounding surveillance technology.

The scorecard is designed for:

- Community organizations
- Nonprofits
- Residents
- Journalists
- Researchers
- Students
- Privacy advocates
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Public officials

It can be used to evaluate technologies including:

- Automated license plate readers (ALPRs)
- Flock Safety camera systems
- Facial recognition
- Surveillance camera networks
- Real-time crime centers
- Drones
- Gunshot detection
- Cell-site simulators
- Biometric systems
- Location-tracking technologies
- Social media monitoring
- Other government surveillance technologies

---

# Why a Scorecard?

Surveillance debates are often reduced to two positions:

> "The technology improves public safety."

or:

> "The technology violates privacy."

Real-world surveillance systems are more complicated.

Two communities may use the same technology while having very different:

- Retention periods
- Search requirements
- Data-sharing rules
- Cybersecurity protections
- Audit requirements
- Public oversight
- Transparency
- Vendor contracts

The purpose of this scorecard is to evaluate those safeguards systematically.

---

# Important: This Is Not a Numerical Privacy Score

This framework intentionally avoids producing a single numerical score such as:

> 72/100 Privacy Score

A single number can create a false sense of scientific precision.

Instead, each safeguard receives an evidence-based rating.

---

# Rating System

Use one of the following ratings for every criterion.

## 🟢 STRONG SAFEGUARD

A meaningful safeguard is clearly documented and appears to address the identified risk.

## 🟡 PARTIAL SAFEGUARD

Some protection exists, but important limitations, exceptions, or unanswered questions remain.

## 🔴 WEAK OR MISSING

The safeguard appears absent, substantially inadequate, or significantly weaker than recommended practices.

## ⚪ UNKNOWN

Available evidence is insufficient to determine whether the safeguard exists.

## 🔵 NOT APPLICABLE

The criterion does not reasonably apply to the technology being evaluated.

---

# Evidence Requirement

Every rating should identify supporting evidence.

Acceptable evidence may include:

- Government policy
- Contract
- Ordinance
- Law
- Audit
- Procurement record
- Meeting record
- Transparency report
- Public-records response
- Technical documentation

Vendor documentation may also be cited, but vendor claims should be clearly labeled.

Do not assign:

> 🟢 STRONG SAFEGUARD

simply because an agency or vendor says a system is "secure," "private," or "responsible."

Identify the actual control.

---

# Evidence Strength

In addition to the safeguard rating, record the strength of the evidence.

## HIGH

Supported by authoritative primary documentation.

Examples:

- Law
- Ordinance
- Signed contract
- Official policy
- Audit

## MODERATE

Supported by official statements or reliable secondary documentation but stronger primary evidence has not been located.

## LOW

Supported primarily by vendor statements, informal statements, or incomplete documentation.

## UNKNOWN

No meaningful evidence has been located.

# Priority Levels

Recommendations may be categorized as:

## 🔴 IMMEDIATE

A significant privacy, cybersecurity, accountability, or governance concern should receive prompt review.

## 🟠 HIGH

A meaningful weakness exists that should be addressed.

## 🟡 MODERATE

Improvement would strengthen governance or transparency.

## 🟢 MAINTAIN

A meaningful safeguard exists and should be preserved.

## ⚪ RESEARCH NEEDED

Insufficient evidence exists to reach a responsible conclusion.

---

# Example Finding

### Data Retention

**Rating:** 🟡 PARTIAL SAFEGUARD

**Evidence Strength:** HIGH

**Evidence:** Agency policy dated [DATE]

**Finding:**

The agency has a documented retention period, which prevents indefinite routine storage.

However, the available policy does not clearly explain who may authorize preservation beyond the standard period or whether preservation activity is audited.

**Recommendation:**

Maintain the defined retention limit while adopting written preservation requirements and audit logging.

---

# Example Unknown

### External Sharing

**Rating:** ⚪ UNKNOWN

**Evidence Strength:** UNKNOWN

**Finding:**

The research team could not locate sufficient records identifying which outside agencies currently have access.

**Recommendation:**

Request existing sharing policies, agreements, configuration records, and available agency-access records.

Notice the difference between:

> "The agency secretly shares everyone's information."

and:

> "Available evidence was insufficient to determine which external organizations currently have access."

The second statement accurately reflects the evidence.

# Responsible Use

Do not manipulate ratings to produce a predetermined conclusion.

If a strong safeguard exists:

**give it credit.**

If evidence is incomplete:

**mark it Unknown.**

If a safeguard exists but has meaningful weaknesses:

**mark it Partial.**

If evidence changes:

**update the assessment.**

Credibility is more valuable than producing the most alarming result.

---

# Review Frequency

A completed scorecard should be reviewed:

- When a contract is renewed
- When policy changes
- When technology expands
- When a new vendor is introduced
- After a significant security incident
- After documented misuse
- When relevant law changes
- At least periodically for active long-term projects

# Core Principle

**Evidence before ratings. Context before conclusions. Transparency before advocacy.**

The scorecard should help communities understand surveillance governance rather than simply reinforce what researchers believed before the assessment began.
