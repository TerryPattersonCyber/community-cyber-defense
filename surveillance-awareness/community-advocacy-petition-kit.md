# Community Advocacy & Petition Kit

A practical set of customizable templates for residents who want to question, limit, regulate, oppose, or seek removal of automated license plate readers (ALPRs), Flock Safety cameras, facial recognition systems, surveillance camera networks, and other surveillance technologies.

This toolkit focuses on lawful civic participation.

Use these templates as starting points and customize them for your community, agency, technology, and concerns.

---

# Before You Advocate

Strong advocacy begins with accurate information.

Before contacting officials, try to determine:

- What technology is being used
- Which agency operates it
- Which company provides it
- How many devices are deployed
- How much the system costs
- What information it collects
- How long information is retained
- Who can access it
- Who can receive shared data
- When the contract expires
- Whether expansion is planned
- Whether the program has been publicly evaluated

Use the [Surveillance Research Toolkit](surveillance-research-toolkit.md) to investigate these questions.

The more specific your request is, the easier it is for officials and other residents to understand what you want changed.

---

# Choose Your Goal

Before organizing, decide what outcome you are seeking.

Possible goals include:

## Stop a Proposed Deployment

Ask officials not to approve a surveillance technology before it is installed.

## Pause a Deployment

Request a temporary pause while privacy, cybersecurity, legal, financial, and community-impact questions are reviewed.

## Prevent Expansion

Oppose adding additional cameras, databases, integrations, or surveillance capabilities.

## Do Not Renew the Contract

Ask officials to allow the current contract to expire.

## Remove Existing Technology

Advocate for termination of the program and removal of existing equipment.

## Create Stronger Safeguards

If removal is not achievable, advocate for policies involving:

- Shorter data retention
- Search justification
- Case-number requirements
- Restricted sharing
- Mandatory multi-factor authentication
- Human verification of alerts
- Audit logs
- Independent audits
- Public transparency reports
- Strong deletion requirements
- Public approval before expansion

---

# Build Your Message

A strong advocacy message usually answers four questions.

### 1. What is happening?

Example:

> Our city currently operates automated license plate readers provided by [VENDOR].

### 2. Why are you concerned?

Example:

> These systems create searchable records containing vehicle, location, and time information, raising questions about retention, sharing, cybersecurity, oversight, and misuse.

### 3. What evidence supports the concern?

Example:

> The current contract permits [RETENTION PERIOD], and the agency's policy allows information to be shared with [AGENCIES].

### 4. What do you want officials to do?

Example:

> I am asking the council not to renew the contract when it expires on [DATE].

Specific requests are usually stronger than simply saying:

> "Do something about surveillance."

- Which outside agencies can access the information
- Cybersecurity protections
- Audit and oversight procedures
- Program cost
- Whether the technology has met its stated goals

[INSERT YOUR REQUEST]

- A public hearing
- Publication of the contract and policies
- An independent audit
- Stronger privacy safeguards

# Template 3: Request to Stop a Proposed Deployment

**Subject: Request to Pause Approval of [TECHNOLOGY]**

Dear [OFFICIAL NAME],

I am asking [CITY/COUNTY] not to approve the proposed deployment of [TECHNOLOGY] until meaningful public review occurs.

Before approval, the public should receive clear information concerning:

- The number and location of proposed devices
- Total program cost
- Contract terms
- Data retention
- Data sharing
- Authorized searches
- Cybersecurity protections
- Auditing
- Policies governing misuse
- Evidence supporting the stated need for the technology

I am requesting that the government delay approval and hold a public hearing before entering into the contract.

Once surveillance infrastructure is deployed, changing or removing it may become more difficult.

Residents deserve an opportunity to evaluate the program before implementation rather than only after it is operating.

Thank you.

# Template 4: Contract Non-Renewal Request

**Subject: Request Not to Renew [VENDOR] Surveillance Contract**

Dear [OFFICIAL / GOVERNMENT BODY],

I am writing regarding the contract between [AGENCY] and [VENDOR] for [TECHNOLOGY].

According to [SOURCE], the current contract expires on [DATE].

I respectfully request that [CITY/COUNTY] not renew this agreement without a comprehensive public review.

Before renewal, officials should evaluate:

- Program effectiveness
- Total cost
- Data retention
- Information sharing
- Cybersecurity
- Search requirements
- Audit results
- Misuse incidents
- Accuracy
- Community concerns

If the program cannot demonstrate adequate safeguards, accountability, necessity, and public benefit, I ask that the contract be allowed to expire and the technology be removed.

At minimum, any proposed renewal should be discussed publicly before a vote occurs.

Thank you.

# Template 7: Three-Minute Public Comment

Good evening.

My name is [NAME], and I am a resident/community member concerned about [TECHNOLOGY] currently used by [AGENCY].

I want to begin by acknowledging that technology can have legitimate public-safety uses.

However, public safety should not prevent us from asking serious questions about privacy, cybersecurity, accuracy, cost, and oversight.

According to [DOCUMENT/SOURCE], our current system [INSERT VERIFIED FACT].

I believe residents deserve answers to several questions.

How long is this information retained?

Who can search it?

Which outside agencies can access it?

Are searches tied to case numbers or documented investigative reasons?

Are all searches logged?

Who audits those logs?

How is misuse detected?

What cybersecurity protections exist?

How has the agency measured whether this system is effective?

Most importantly, what opportunity does the public have to decide whether this technology should continue?

I am asking the council to [SPECIFIC REQUEST].

That might mean holding a public hearing, publishing the contract, commissioning an audit, adopting stronger privacy protections, pausing expansion, or declining to renew the contract.

Technology should not become permanent simply because it was purchased once.

Residents deserve ongoing oversight of systems capable of collecting information about their movements and activities.

Thank you.

# Template 11: Request for Stronger Data-Retention Rules

**Subject: Request to Reduce Surveillance Data Retention**

Dear [OFFICIAL NAME],

I am requesting that [AGENCY] reduce the retention period for information collected through [TECHNOLOGY].

The current retention period is:

[CURRENT PERIOD]

I am asking officials to consider:

[REQUESTED PERIOD]

Shorter retention can reduce the size of historical databases while still allowing information relevant to legitimate investigations to be preserved when legally appropriate.

I also request clear policies explaining:

- Who can preserve records beyond the normal period
- What justification is required
- How long preserved records remain available
- Whether preservation actions are logged
- What happens to retained information when the contract ends

Thank you.

# Template 12: Request to Restrict Data Sharing

**Subject: Request for Stronger Surveillance Data-Sharing Restrictions**

Dear [OFFICIAL NAME],

I am requesting that [AGENCY] publicly review and restrict how information collected through [TECHNOLOGY] is shared.

Please consider policies requiring:

- Explicit approval before new sharing relationships
- Restrictions on out-of-state sharing
- Clear rules governing federal access
- Documented purposes for external searches
- Audit logging
- Periodic review of sharing partners
- Public reporting identifying participating organizations where legally permissible

Residents should be able to understand how locally collected information may become accessible outside the local agency.

Thank you.

# Template 13: Request for Mandatory Search Justification

**Subject: Request for Search Accountability Requirements**

Dear [OFFICIAL NAME],

I am asking [AGENCY] to require documented justification for every search of [SURVEILLANCE DATABASE].

At minimum, searches should include:

- Individual authenticated user
- Search date and time
- Search reason
- Case or incident identifier when applicable
- Agency performing the search

Search records should be retained for auditing.

These requirements can help identify misuse and demonstrate whether database access is consistent with agency policy.

Thank you.

# Template 16: Request for Surveillance Ordinance

Some communities may choose to adopt local laws or ordinances requiring public approval before surveillance technology is acquired or expanded.

**Subject: Request for Surveillance Technology Oversight Ordinance**

Dear [OFFICIAL NAME],

I am asking [CITY/COUNTY] to consider adopting an ordinance requiring meaningful public oversight of surveillance technology.

A policy could require:

1. Public notice before acquisition.
2. Publication of proposed use policies.
3. Privacy and cybersecurity assessments.
4. Public hearings.
5. Approval by the elected governing body.
6. Data-retention rules.
7. Data-sharing rules.
8. Audit requirements.
9. Annual transparency reporting.
10. Public approval before substantial expansion.

The purpose would not be to prohibit every technology automatically.

The purpose would be to ensure that technologies capable of collecting sensitive information receive public review before becoming permanent infrastructure.

Thank you.

# Talking Points for Meetings With Officials

When meeting with an elected official, avoid trying to explain everything at once.

Focus on a few clear points.

## Transparency

> Residents should be able to understand what technology exists and how it is governed.

## Retention

> Information should not be stored longer than necessary without a legitimate reason.

## Sharing

> Local information should not quietly become accessible to an unlimited network of outside organizations.

## Cybersecurity

> Databases containing sensitive information deserve strong authentication, access controls, logging, and incident-response protections.

## Accountability

> Searches should be documented and auditable.

## Effectiveness

> Programs should demonstrate whether they achieved the goals used to justify their purchase.

## Public Approval

> Major surveillance expansions should receive meaningful public review.

# Questions to Ask Officials

Ask specific questions such as:

1. How many cameras are currently operating?
2. How much does the program cost annually?
3. When does the contract expire?
4. Is renewal automatic?
5. What is the retention period?
6. Can the retention period be changed administratively?
7. Who can search the database?
8. Is a case number required?
9. Are all searches logged?
10. Who audits the search logs?
11. Which outside organizations receive access?
12. Can out-of-state agencies search locally collected information?
13. Can federal agencies access the system?
14. Is MFA mandatory?
15. Have any users been disciplined for misuse?
16. Has the system experienced a security incident?
17. How is effectiveness measured?
18. Has the program undergone independent review?
19. Is expansion planned?
20. When will the public next have an opportunity to vote or comment on the program?

# Organizing a Community Meeting

A community meeting does not need to be complicated.

Suggested agenda:

## 1. Explain the Technology

Provide a short factual explanation.

## 2. Present What You Found

Share:

- Contract
- Cost
- Retention
- Sharing
- Policies
- Audit information

## 3. Identify Unanswered Questions

Do not speculate.

Clearly identify information that has not been verified.

## 4. Discuss Community Goals

Possible goals:

- More transparency
- Stronger restrictions
- Public hearing
- Pause
- Non-renewal
- Removal

## 5. Assign Tasks

Examples:

- Public-records research
- Meeting attendance
- Petition organization
- Official outreach
- Community education
- Media outreach

## 6. Set a Deadline

Contract renewal dates and government meeting schedules can determine how quickly advocacy must occur.

# Petition Collection Tips

Before circulating a petition:

- Clearly identify the government body being addressed.
- State the requested action.
- Avoid exaggerated claims.
- Provide a link to supporting documentation.
- Consider collecting city or ZIP code information to demonstrate local participation.
- Protect unnecessary personal information.
- Explain how signatures will be used.

Online petitions can demonstrate support, but direct outreach to decision-makers should continue alongside the petition.

# Contact Local Media

Local journalists may be interested when surveillance technology involves:

- Public money
- Government contracts
- Privacy
- New technology
- Public opposition
- Misuse
- Data sharing
- Contract renewal
- Lack of transparency

When contacting a reporter:

- Keep the initial message short.
- Provide documentation.
- Link to contracts and policies.
- Explain the local connection.
- Identify the upcoming decision or deadline.
- Avoid making claims you cannot document.

# Social Media Advocacy

Social media can help educate residents, but accuracy matters.

A useful post includes:

1. A verified fact
2. The original source
3. Why it matters
4. The requested action
5. Meeting or petition information

Example:

> Our city council will vote on renewing the city's ALPR contract on [DATE]. The current agreement covers [NUMBER] cameras at an annual cost of [COST]. Residents can review the contract here: [LINK]. Public comment begins at [TIME]. I'm asking the council to delay renewal until retention, sharing, and audit policies receive public review.

Avoid turning uncertain information into fact simply because it circulates widely online.

- Neighborhood associations

- Cybersecurity professionals
- Community organizations
- Student organizations
- Journalists

They only need enough common ground around the specific surveillance policy being addressed.

# Keep Advocacy Evidence-Based

Separate:

## Documented Facts

Supported by contracts, policies, records, or reliable sources.

## Opinions

Your interpretation of whether the technology is appropriate.

## Advocacy Goals

The policy change you want.

For example:

**Fact:**  
The contract expires December 31.

**Opinion:**  
The program creates excessive privacy risk.

**Advocacy goal:**  
The city should not renew the contract.

Clearly separating these categories strengthens credibility.

# If Officials Do Not Respond

Consider:

1. Following up respectfully.
2. Contacting another representative.
3. Speaking during public comment.
4. Asking that the issue be added to an agenda.
5. Organizing additional residents.
6. Contacting local journalists.
7. Requesting additional public records.
8. Contacting civil-liberties or legal organizations.
9. Following upcoming elections and policy debates.
10. Continuing to document the issue publicly.

Advocacy often requires more than one communication.

# If Full Removal Is Not Achievable

Advocacy can still produce meaningful safeguards.

Possible intermediate goals include:

- Prevent expansion
- Shorten retention
- Restrict sharing
- Require case numbers
- Require documented search purposes
- Require MFA
- Require regular audits
- Require annual reporting
- Require human verification
- Restrict searches involving sensitive activities where legally permissible
- Require public approval before future expansion
- Add contract termination protections
- Require deletion after contract termination

Policy improvements do not prevent residents from continuing to advocate for removal later.

# Lawful Advocacy Only

Do not:

- Damage cameras
- Cover or obstruct cameras unlawfully
- Cut wires
- Interfere with electrical systems
- Trespass
- Attempt to access camera hardware
- Hack surveillance systems
- Steal equipment
- Threaten officials or employees
- Harass individual workers

Use lawful civic processes such as:

- Research
- Public records
- Public comments
- Petitions
- Peaceful demonstrations
- Journalism
- Elections
- Legislation
- Litigation through qualified legal organizations
- Community education

Protecting civil liberties does not require committing crimes.

- [ ] Identify the technology.

- [ ] Review the policy.
- [ ] Determine retention.
- [ ] Determine sharing.
- [ ] Review available audit information.

- [ ] Define the specific requested action.
- [ ] Identify who has authority to make the change.
- [ ] Identify the relevant deadline.

## Outreach

- [ ] Contact elected officials.
- [ ] Attend public meetings.
- [ ] Submit public comments.
- [ ] Contact community organizations.
- [ ] Contact journalists where appropriate.

## Organizing

- [ ] Create educational materials.
- [ ] Start a petition if useful.
- [ ] Organize community members.
- [ ] Track responses.
- [ ] Document government decisions.

## Follow-Through

- [ ] Attend the final vote.
- [ ] Record the outcome.
- [ ] Verify that promised policy changes occur.
- [ ] Continue monitoring contract renewals.
- [ ] Publish findings responsibly.
