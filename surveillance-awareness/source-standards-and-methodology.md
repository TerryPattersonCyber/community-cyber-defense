# Source Standards & Research Methodology

The Community Cyber Defense Project uses a documented research methodology to improve accuracy, transparency, reproducibility, and long-term reliability.

This methodology applies to surveillance-related:

- Guides
- Community audits
- Policy scorecards
- Research reports
- Educational materials
- Advocacy materials
- Technology explainers
- Public-records research
- Community profiles
- Website tools
- Future databases

The goal is simple:

> Readers should be able to understand not only what the project says, but why it says it, what evidence supports it, how current that evidence is, and what remains unknown.

---

# Core Research Principles

## 1. Evidence Before Conclusions

Research should begin with evidence rather than a predetermined conclusion.

A researcher may have legitimate concerns about a surveillance technology.

However, findings should still distinguish between:

- What is documented
- What is claimed
- What is inferred
- What is disputed
- What remains unknown

---

## 2. Primary Sources First

Whenever practical, important factual claims should be traced to the original source.

Examples include:

- Laws
- Ordinances
- Government policies
- Contracts
- Procurement documents
- Public meeting records
- Audit reports
- Court documents
- Official technical documentation
- Public-records responses

Secondary sources can provide important context, investigation, or analysis but should not automatically replace available primary evidence.

---

## 3. Source Perspective Matters

Every source has a role and perspective.

A source may be:

- Government
- Vendor
- Technical standards organization
- Academic researcher
- Journalist
- Civil-liberties organization
- Advocacy organization
- Community researcher
- Private individual

Readers should know what kind of source supports a claim.

---

## 4. Unknown Is a Valid Finding

Researchers should not guess when information cannot be verified.

Use:

> Unknown

when evidence is unavailable or insufficient.

An unanswered question may itself demonstrate a transparency gap.

---

## 5. Separate Fact From Analysis

For example:

### FACT

> The contract expires on December 31, 2027.

### ANALYSIS

> The upcoming expiration creates an opportunity for public review.

### ADVOCACY POSITION

> Our organization believes the contract should not be renewed.

Keeping these separate improves credibility.

---

# Source Hierarchy

The following hierarchy can help determine which sources should receive the greatest evidentiary weight.

It is a guide, not an absolute rule.

---

# Tier 1 — Primary Authoritative Sources

Generally preferred for factual claims.

Examples:

- Statutes
- Regulations
- Ordinances
- Court decisions
- Signed contracts
- Government policies
- Procurement documents
- Government audit reports
- Official meeting minutes
- Agency records
- Public-records responses
- Official technical standards

Example organizations:

- State legislatures
- Local governments
- Courts
- NIST
- CISA
- Federal agencies

---

# Tier 2 — Original Technical or Organizational Sources

Useful for understanding what a technology provider or organization says about its own system.

Examples:

- Vendor technical documentation
- Vendor privacy policies
- Vendor security documentation
- Product documentation
- Transparency centers
- Terms of service
- Data-processing agreements

These sources are useful but should be clearly identified as vendor-provided information.

---

# Tier 3 — Independent Research

Examples:

- Peer-reviewed research
- University research
- Independent technical analysis
- Research institutes
- Reputable nonprofit research
- Security research

These sources can provide analysis that may not exist in official documentation.

Researchers should review:

- Methodology
- Sample size
- Limitations
- Publication date
- Applicability

---

# Tier 4 — Investigative & Professional Journalism

High-quality journalism can uncover information that would otherwise remain difficult to identify.

Useful reporting may include:

- Public-records investigations
- Contract analysis
- Interviews
- Security incidents
- Misuse cases
- Vendor relationships
- Government decisions

Whenever possible, review the underlying documents referenced by the reporting.

---

# Tier 5 — Advocacy Organizations

Advocacy organizations can provide valuable:

- Policy analysis
- Legal research
- Civil-liberties analysis
- Litigation information
- Organizing resources
- Model legislation
- Community research

Examples include organizations focused on:

- Privacy
- Civil rights
- Civil liberties
- Government accountability
- Technology policy

Their perspective should be clearly recognized.

---

# Tier 6 — Community & Informal Sources

Examples:

- Community websites
- Social media
- Online forums
- Personal accounts
- Unverified databases
- Anonymous tips

These sources may identify useful leads.

They should generally not be treated as authoritative evidence without additional verification.

---

# Claim Classification System

Important findings should be classified.

Use one of these labels.

---

## VERIFIED

Supported by authoritative or reliable primary evidence.

Example:

> VERIFIED — The signed contract states that the agreement expires July 1, 2027.

---

## REPORTED

Supported by credible journalism or secondary reporting, but the primary record has not yet been located.

Example:

> REPORTED — Local journalists reported that approximately 40 cameras were deployed.

---

## VENDOR CLAIM

Stated by the technology provider.

Example:

> VENDOR CLAIM — The company states that customer data is encrypted at rest.

---

## AGENCY CLAIM

Stated by the government agency but not independently verified.

Example:

> AGENCY CLAIM — The police department states that the system has assisted in 120 investigations.

---

## ADVOCACY CLAIM

Stated by an advocacy organization.

Example:

> ADVOCACY CLAIM — The organization argues that regional data sharing creates significant privacy concerns.

---

## ANALYSIS

A conclusion derived from documented evidence.

Example:

> ANALYSIS — Because renewal does not require another public vote under the current agreement, opportunities for public review may be limited.

---

## UNVERIFIED

Information has been identified but cannot yet be adequately confirmed.

---

## UNKNOWN

No reliable answer has been located.
