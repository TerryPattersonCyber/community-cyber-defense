# Model Surveillance Safeguards Library

A practical library of policy, cybersecurity, privacy, accountability, procurement, and oversight safeguards that communities may consider when evaluating surveillance technology.

This resource is designed for:

- Residents
- Nonprofits
- Community organizations
- Journalists
- Researchers
- Students
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Privacy advocates
- Public officials
- Policy staff

The purpose of this library is not to prescribe one universal surveillance policy.

Instead, it provides a structured set of safeguards that communities can evaluate based on:

- Technology
- Local law
- Community priorities
- Government structure
- Risk
- Cost
- Public-safety objectives
- Privacy
- Cybersecurity
- Civil-liberties considerations

---

# How to Use This Library

Safeguards can be used when:

- Reviewing an existing program
- Drafting policy
- Evaluating a contract
- Preparing public comments
- Comparing jurisdictions
- Developing recommendations
- Reviewing a proposed deployment
- Preparing for contract renewal

Each safeguard should be considered in context.

A safeguard listed here is not automatically:

- Legally required
- Technically necessary
- Appropriate for every technology
- Sufficient on its own

# CATEGORY 1 — PUBLIC TRANSPARENCY

## Safeguard: Public Technology Inventory

Government publishes a list of surveillance technologies it operates.

Possible fields:

- Technology
- Vendor
- Agency
- Purpose
- Deployment status
- Policy
- Contract
- Cost
- Retention
- Last reviewed

### Why It Matters

Residents cannot meaningfully evaluate a system they do not know exists.

---

## Safeguard: Public Policy

The governing policy is publicly available.

### Stronger Version

Policy is:

- Easy to locate
- Written in plain language
- Versioned
- Dated
- Updated when materially changed

---

## Safeguard: Public Contract

The current contract and relevant amendments are publicly available where permitted by law.

### Stronger Version

Government also publishes:

- Pricing
- Statement of work
- Amendments
- Renewal documents
- Data-processing terms

---

## Safeguard: Public Cost Information

Government publishes:

- Initial cost
- Annual cost
- Funding source
- Recurring subscription cost

---

## Safeguard: Transparency Portal

A public portal provides current program information.

Possible content:

- Program overview
- Policy
- Retention
- Sharing
- Device count
- Audit information
- Contact information
- Contract information

# CATEGORY 2 — PUBLIC APPROVAL

## Safeguard: Approval Before Acquisition

Deployment requires approval by the appropriate elected or governing body.

### Stronger Version

Approval occurs only after:

- Public notice
- Published documentation
- Opportunity for comment
- Cost analysis
- Privacy review
- Security review

---

## Safeguard: Public Hearing

A public hearing occurs before significant deployment.

### Stronger Version

Public materials are available in advance so residents have meaningful time to review them.

---

## Safeguard: Approval Before Material Expansion

Additional approval is required before:

- Significant device expansion
- New analytics
- New vendor
- New database integration
- New sharing network
- Materially new purpose

---

# CATEGORY 3 — PURPOSE LIMITATION

## Safeguard: Defined Purpose

Policy clearly states the authorized purpose of the technology.

Avoid vague language such as:

> Any legitimate government purpose.

Prefer specific categories of authorized use.

---

## Safeguard: Authorized Use List

Policy identifies when the technology may be used.

---

## Safeguard: Prohibited Use List

Policy identifies prohibited purposes.

Examples may include uses inconsistent with law, policy, or stated community objectives.

---

## Safeguard: New-Use Approval

Materially new uses require review before implementation.

This helps reduce mission creep.

---

# CATEGORY 4 — DATA MINIMIZATION

## Safeguard: Defined Data Collection

Policy clearly identifies what the system collects.

---

## Safeguard: Minimum Necessary Collection

Collect only information reasonably necessary for the stated purpose.

---

## Safeguard: Disable Unnecessary Features

Features not needed for approved purposes remain disabled.

Examples may include:

- Additional analytics
- Biometric functionality
- Extended retention
- Cross-system integrations

---

## Safeguard: Feature Inventory

Government maintains a record of enabled system capabilities.

---

# CATEGORY 5 — RETENTION

## Safeguard: Defined Retention Period

Data has a clearly documented default retention period.

---

## Safeguard: Limited Retention

Routine data is not retained indefinitely.

Shorter retention may reduce:

- Privacy exposure
- Breach impact
- Misuse opportunity
- Storage burden

Retention should still account for legitimate operational and legal requirements.

---

## Safeguard: Automatic Deletion

Data is deleted automatically when the retention period expires.

---

## Safeguard: Preservation Justification

Keeping records beyond the normal retention period requires documented justification.

---

## Safeguard: Preservation Logging

Preservation activity is logged.

---

## Safeguard: Retention Change Review

Material increases in retention require review and documentation.

---

# CATEGORY 6 — SEARCH ACCOUNTABILITY

## Safeguard: Individual User Accounts

Each authorized user has an individual account.

Avoid shared credentials where possible.

---

## Safeguard: Search Purpose

Users must provide a documented reason for a search.

---

## Safeguard: Case or Incident Reference

Where appropriate, searches should be tied to:

- Case number
- Incident number
- Call-for-service
- Investigative reference

---

## Safeguard: Search Logging

Every search is logged.

---

## Safeguard: Search Review

Supervisors or compliance staff periodically review search activity.

---

## Safeguard: Unusual Search Detection

Systems or reviewers identify unusual patterns that may warrant additional review.

---

# Recommendation Builder

When identifying a weakness, connect it to a specific safeguard.

Example:

## Finding

External data sharing is not clearly documented.

## Possible Safeguards

- Adopt written sharing policy.
- Maintain approved partner list.
- Require approval before new sharing relationships.
- Restrict redisclosure.
- Audit external access.
- Publish high-level sharing information.

---

# Example: Retention

## Finding

The system retains routine information for an extended period.

## Possible Safeguards

- Reduce default retention.
- Require documented preservation.
- Log preservation activity.
- Publish retention policy.
- Require review before retention increases.

---

# Example: Search Accountability

## Finding

Users can search the database without documenting a purpose.

## Possible Safeguards

- Require search justification.
- Require case or incident identifier where appropriate.
- Log searches.
- Conduct supervisory review.
- Review unusual activity.

---

# Example: Cybersecurity

## Finding

Available evidence does not establish whether MFA is mandatory.

## Possible Response

Before recommending a policy change:

1. Request current authentication policy.
2. Determine whether MFA exists.
3. If absent, evaluate mandatory MFA as a potential safeguard.

Unknown should drive research before becoming a factual claim.

# Policy Drafting Principles

When turning safeguards into policy language:

## Be Specific

Avoid:

> Appropriate security shall be used.

Prefer requirements that identify specific controls where appropriate.

---

## Define Responsibility

Identify:

- Who approves
- Who reviews
- Who audits
- Who reports

---

## Define Frequency

Instead of:

> Audits occur regularly.

consider:

> Search activity is reviewed at least [defined interval].

Exact requirements should match local needs and law.

---

## Define Exceptions

Policies should explain:

- When exceptions exist
- Who approves them
- How they are documented

---

## Define Enforcement

A safeguard without accountability may be difficult to enforce.

---

# Technical Safeguard vs. Policy Safeguard

Some protections are technical.

Example:

> MFA enabled.

Others are procedural.

Example:

> Supervisor reviews search logs monthly.

Strong governance may require both.

---

# Preventing "Paper Safeguards"

A written policy does not automatically prove the safeguard exists in practice.

to compare:

**Policy → Implementation → Evidence**

# Community Use

Residents can use safeguards to turn a general concern into a specific question.

Instead of:

> This system has no accountability.

ask:

> Does the agency conduct recurring audits of search logs, and are any audit findings publicly available?

Specific questions are easier to research.
