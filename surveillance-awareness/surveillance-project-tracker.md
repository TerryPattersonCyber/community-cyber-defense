# Surveillance Project Tracker

A reusable project-management framework for organizing long-term surveillance research, public-records requests, policy reviews, contract deadlines, public meetings, advocacy, and follow-up.

This tracker is designed for:

- Nonprofits
- Community organizations
- Journalists
- Researchers
- Students
- Privacy advocates
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Civic technology organizations
- Public officials
- Volunteer research teams

The goal is to make surveillance research sustainable over months or years rather than dependent on one person remembering what happened.

---

# Why a Project Tracker Matters

Surveillance research often involves:

- Multiple agencies
- Multiple technologies
- Long contracts
- Public-records requests
- Policy changes
- Budget cycles
- Public meetings
- Audit requests
- Vendor documents
- Contract renewals
- Research assignments
- Community events
- Corrections
- Follow-up research

Without a structured tracker, important information can be lost.

A strong tracker should answer:

> What are we researching?

> What have we completed?

> What remains unknown?

> Who is responsible?

> What deadlines are coming?

> What changed?

> What happened after we acted?

- Review policy
- Update surveillance audit

# Impact Classification

Avoid attributing every change directly to project activity.

Use:

## DIRECTLY DOCUMENTED

Decision-maker explicitly connects change to the project or recommendation.

## CONTRIBUTING FACTOR

Evidence suggests the project contributed among other factors.

## TEMPORALLY RELATED

Change occurred after project activity but causation is not established.

## UNRELATED / UNKNOWN

No credible connection established.
