# Contribution & Community Submission Guide

A framework for contributing research, records, corrections, community surveillance findings, and improvements to the Community Cyber Defense Project.

This guide is designed for:

- Residents
- Community organizations
- Nonprofits
- Researchers
- Journalists
- Students
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Privacy advocates
- Civic technology organizations
- Public officials
- Other contributors

The goal is to make community participation possible while protecting the accuracy, privacy, security, neutrality, and credibility of the project.

---

# Why Community Contributions Matter

Surveillance technologies are deployed across many different communities.

No single researcher or organization can realistically monitor every:

- City
- County
- Agency
- Contract
- Vendor
- Policy
- Public meeting
- Technology deployment
- Contract renewal
- Policy change

Community contributions can help expand the project while allowing local researchers to contribute knowledge about their own communities.

However:

> More submissions do not automatically create better research.

Every contribution should follow documented evidence and verification standards.

---

# Core Contribution Principle

**Contribute evidence, not assumptions.**

A submission should make it possible for another researcher to understand:

1. What is being claimed?
2. What evidence supports the claim?
3. Where did the evidence come from?
4. When was it verified?
5. What remains unknown?
6. Can another researcher reproduce the finding?

# What Should Not Be Submitted?

Do not submit:

- Passwords
- Authentication tokens
- Private credentials
- Stolen credentials
- Exploit instructions
- Malware
- Instructions for damaging surveillance equipment
- Instructions for disabling surveillance infrastructure
- Instructions for unauthorized access
- Unnecessary personal information
- Doxxing material
- Unverified accusations against individuals
- Private medical information
- Financial account information
- Sensitive personal records unrelated to legitimate research

---

# Security-Sensitive Information

A record being publicly obtainable does not automatically mean every technical detail should be republished.

Submissions containing information about:

- Credentials
- Internal network architecture
- Security vulnerabilities
- Sensitive system configurations
- Administrative interfaces
- Authentication mechanisms
- Internal infrastructure
- Detailed physical security information

may require additional review.

The project may:

- Redact information
- Summarize information
- Delay publication
- Decline publication

when unnecessary disclosure could create meaningful security or privacy risks.

---

# Personal Information

Community research should focus primarily on:

- Government systems
- Public agencies
- Vendors
- Contracts
- Policies
- Public decisions
- Technical safeguards
- Public records

rather than collecting personal information about private individuals.

---

# Data Minimization

Before submitting personal information, ask:

> Is this information actually necessary to support the research finding?

If not:

Do not submit it.

---

# Public Officials

Publicly relevant professional information may be included when necessary.

Examples:

- Name
- Government title
- Agency
- Official email
- Public meeting statements

Avoid publishing unnecessary personal information.

# Source Classification

Submissions should identify source type.

Possible classifications:

## PRIMARY GOVERNMENT SOURCE

Official government material.

## CONTRACTUAL SOURCE

Contract or procurement material.

## VENDOR SOURCE

Vendor documentation or statement.

## ACADEMIC SOURCE

Research produced through an academic institution or scholarly publication.

## JOURNALISTIC SOURCE

Credible reporting.

## ADVOCACY SOURCE

Material produced by an advocacy organization.

## COMMUNITY SOURCE

Locally produced research or documentation.

## OTHER

Source does not fit another category.

---

# Claim Labels

Use:

## VERIFIED

Supported by reliable primary evidence.

## REPORTED

Supported by credible secondary evidence.

## AGENCY CLAIM

Stated by a government agency.

## VENDOR CLAIM

Stated by a vendor.

## ADVOCACY CLAIM

Stated by an advocacy organization.

## ANALYSIS

Researcher interpretation based on evidence.

## UNVERIFIED

Not adequately confirmed.

## UNKNOWN

Reliable information has not been located.

---

# Evidence Strength

Where useful, classify evidence as:

## HIGH

Strong primary evidence directly supporting the finding.

## MODERATE

Credible evidence supports the finding but important limitations exist.

## LOW

Evidence is limited, indirect, or incomplete.

## UNKNOWN

Evidence quality cannot yet be established.

# Required Citation Information

Whenever possible record:

- Author or organization
- Document title
- Publication date
- URL
- Date accessed
- Page number
- Section
- Relevant quotation or summary
- Source classification
- Verification date

---

# Original Documents

When submitting a document, preserve the original version whenever possible.

Do not modify the original evidence file.

If annotations are needed:

Create a separate working copy.

---

# File Integrity

For important records, contributors may record:

- Original filename
- Download date
- Source URL
- File size
- Cryptographic hash

Hashes can help establish whether a stored file has changed.

---

# Screenshot Submissions

Screenshots may support research but should not automatically replace the underlying source.

Whenever possible provide:

- Screenshot
- Original URL
- Date
- Context

Screenshots without context may be insufficient for verification.

---

# Social Media Sources

Social-media posts may sometimes document:

- Government announcements
- Vendor statements
- Public statements
- Program changes

When possible:

1. Preserve the post.
2. Record the account.
3. Record the date.
4. Record the URL.
5. Search for supporting primary documentation.

A social-media post should not automatically establish broader claims beyond what the post actually states.

---

# Anonymous Submissions

Future versions of the project may allow anonymous submissions.

Anonymous submissions may be useful when a contributor has legitimate privacy concerns.

However:

> Anonymous does not mean unverified.

Evidence must still be reviewed.

---

# Confidential Sources

The public Community Cyber Defense repository is not designed to function as a secure whistleblower submission platform.

Do not use ordinary project submission channels for highly sensitive confidential materials.

Organizations requiring secure source communication should establish appropriate systems and professional procedures.

Evaluate the evidence.

Vendors may submit:

- Product documentation

---

# Contributor Conflict Disclosure

Contributors should disclose relevant relationships that could reasonably affect interpretation of their research.

Examples:

- Employment by the agency
- Employment by the vendor
- Vendor consulting
- Litigation
- Campaign involvement
- Advocacy organization role
- Research funding

Disclosure provides context.

It does not automatically invalidate the contribution.

---

# Contributor Conduct

Contributors should:

- Treat other contributors professionally
- Cite evidence
- Correct mistakes
- Respect privacy
- Avoid harassment
- Avoid doxxing
- Avoid knowingly false submissions
- Avoid manipulating evidence
- Avoid impersonation

---

# Evidence Manipulation

The project should reject intentionally manipulated evidence.

Examples:

- Altered documents presented as originals
- Misleading edits
- Fabricated screenshots
- False quotations
- Invented sources
- AI-generated material presented as original evidence

---

# AI-Generated Submissions

AI may assist with:

- Summarization
- Organization
- Drafting
- Document classification
- Research planning

AI output is not primary evidence.

Important claims should be verified against original sources.

---

# AI Disclosure

When AI materially contributed to research or drafting, contributors should disclose its role where appropriate.

Example:

> AI-assisted tools were used to organize source notes. Factual findings were manually verified against the cited records.

---

# Automated Data Collection

Future contributors may use automated tools to collect publicly available information.

Automated collection should:

- Follow applicable law
- Respect relevant access rules
- Avoid bypassing security controls
- Avoid unauthorized access
- Minimize unnecessary personal information
- Preserve source provenance

---

# Submission Review

Each submission should be reviewed for:

## Relevance

Does it relate to the project?

## Source Quality

Is the source identifiable?

## Verification

Can the claim be checked?

## Currency

Is the information current?

## Privacy

Does it expose unnecessary personal information?

## Security

Could publication create unnecessary security risk?

## Context

Is the evidence represented accurately?

## Completeness

Are important limitations disclosed?

---

# Review Outcomes

A submission may be:

## ACCEPTED

Ready for publication.

## ACCEPTED WITH CHANGES

Useful but requires correction or formatting.

## NEEDS MORE EVIDENCE

Additional evidence required.

## DUPLICATE

Already documented.

## ARCHIVED

Useful historical information.

## DECLINED

Does not meet project standards.
