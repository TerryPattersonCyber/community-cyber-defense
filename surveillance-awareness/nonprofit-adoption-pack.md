# Nonprofit Adoption Pack

A practical implementation guide for nonprofits, community organizations, research groups, civic technology teams, and public-interest organizations that want to adopt the Community Surveillance Audit System.

This pack is designed to help an organization move from:

> “This framework looks useful.”

to:

> “We have a repeatable surveillance research and accountability program our staff or volunteers can actually run.”

---

# Purpose

The Community Surveillance Audit System is intended to be reusable.

Organizations should be able to adapt it for:

- Local surveillance research
- Public education
- Community workshops
- Contract review
- Policy analysis
- Public-records research
- Cybersecurity review
- Journalism support
- Community reporting
- Civic participation
- Long-term monitoring

Organizations do not need to adopt a specific political position in order to use the framework.

The methodology is designed to support evidence-based research regardless of whether an organization ultimately:

- Supports a surveillance program
- Opposes it
- Recommends safeguards
- Requests additional transparency
- Requests an audit
- Opposes expansion
- Opposes renewal
- Requests termination

---

# Adoption Principles

Organizations using the framework should maintain five core standards.

## 1. Evidence

Important claims should be tied to identifiable sources.

## 2. Transparency

Readers should understand how conclusions were reached.

## 3. Uncertainty

Unknown information should remain clearly labeled.

## 4. Privacy

Research should avoid unnecessary collection or publication of personal information.

## 5. Corrections

Organizations should correct meaningful errors when credible new evidence becomes available.

# Adoption Levels

Organizations may adopt the system at different levels depending on capacity.

---

# LEVEL 1 — COMMUNITY STARTER

Best for:

- Small nonprofits
- Neighborhood groups
- Volunteer groups
- Student organizations
- Local coalitions

Typical team:

**1–3 people**

Primary activities:

- Learn surveillance basics
- Identify local technology
- Locate public policies
- Locate contracts
- Track important dates
- Submit basic records requests
- Prepare a community summary

Recommended resources:

1. Surveillance Technology Awareness Guide
2. Surveillance Glossary
3. Surveillance Research Toolkit
4. Community Surveillance Audit
5. Community Surveillance Report Template

---

# LEVEL 2 — STRUCTURED RESEARCH PROGRAM

Best for organizations with:

- Dedicated staff
- Recurring volunteers
- Policy programs
- Research capacity

Typical team:

**3–8 people**

Additional capabilities:

- Contract review
- Policy scorecards
- Public-records tracking
- Cybersecurity review
- Evidence logs
- Community workshops
- Government meeting monitoring
- Periodic reports

Recommended resources:

- Surveillance Contract Review Toolkit
- Surveillance Policy Scorecard
- Model Surveillance Safeguards Library
- Surveillance Project Tracker
- Source Standards & Research Methodology
- Nonprofit & Community Organizer Toolkit

---

# LEVEL 3 — ORGANIZATIONAL SURVEILLANCE PROGRAM

Best for:

- Larger nonprofits
- Civil-liberties organizations
- Civic technology groups
- Research organizations
- Journalism collaborations
- Multi-community projects

Typical team:

**5+ people**

Possible capabilities:

- Multiple local surveillance projects
- Dedicated research staff
- Technical review
- Policy analysis
- Community training
- Public reporting
- Contract monitoring
- Long-term project maintenance
- Cross-community comparisons
- Organization-branded reports

---
