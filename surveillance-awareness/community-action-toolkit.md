# Community Action Toolkit: Automated License Plate Readers & Surveillance Cameras

A practical guide for community members who want to question, limit, regulate, or oppose the use of automated license plate readers (ALPRs) and other surveillance technologies in their communities.

This toolkit focuses on lawful civic participation, government transparency, public education, and policy advocacy.

---

# 1. Learn Who Controls the System

Before organizing against a surveillance system, determine who authorized and operates it.

Possible organizations include:

- City government
- County government
- Police or sheriff's departments
- State agencies
- Transportation departments
- Homeowners associations
- Private businesses
- Schools or universities

Try to determine:

- Who approved the cameras?
- Who pays for them?
- Which company provides the technology?
- How many cameras exist?
- Where are they located?
- When was the program approved?
- When does the contract expire?
- How much does the program cost?

Understanding who controls the system helps determine who has the authority to change it.

---

# 2. Find the Contract

Government surveillance technology is often purchased through contracts that may contain important information about how the system operates.

Look for:

- Vendor name
- Contract cost
- Contract expiration date
- Renewal provisions
- Number of cameras
- Data retention periods
- Data-sharing provisions
- Authorized users
- Audit requirements
- Security requirements

Contracts may sometimes be available through:

- City or county websites
- Meeting agendas
- Meeting minutes
- Procurement records
- Public-records requests

Knowing when a contract expires can be especially important because residents may be able to advocate against renewal.

---

# 3. Use Public-Records Laws

Public-records requests can help residents understand how government surveillance technology is being used.

Depending on applicable law, potentially useful records may include:

- ALPR contracts
- Vendor agreements
- Purchase orders
- Policies
- Data-retention policies
- Data-sharing agreements
- Training materials
- Audit logs
- Usage statistics
- Communications with vendors
- Communications about purchasing the system

A request should be specific enough that the agency can identify the records being requested.

---

# Sample Public-Records Request

**Subject: Public Records Request — Automated License Plate Reader Program**

To Whom It May Concern:

I am requesting public records related to the agency's use of automated license plate reader technology.

Please provide available records concerning:

1. Current contracts or agreements involving automated license plate reader systems.
2. Policies governing the use of ALPR technology.
3. Policies governing retention and deletion of ALPR data.
4. Agreements or policies concerning sharing ALPR information with other agencies or organizations.
5. The number of ALPR cameras currently operated or funded by the agency.
6. Records showing the cost of the program.
7. Available audit or access logs showing how the system has been used, subject to applicable public-records law.
8. Training materials provided to personnel authorized to use the system.

Electronic copies are preferred when available.

Thank you.

---

# 4. Attend Local Government Meetings

City councils, county commissions, oversight boards, and other government bodies frequently provide opportunities for public comment.

Residents can use these meetings to:

- Ask how surveillance systems are being used
- Request transparency
- Question contract renewals
- Request independent audits
- Ask for stronger privacy protections
- Advocate for removal of the technology
- Request public hearings before expansion

Before attending:

1. Find the meeting schedule.
2. Review the agenda.
3. Determine the public-comment rules.
4. Learn the speaking time limit.
5. Prepare a short statement.

---

# Sample Public Comment

Good evening.

I am asking the council to review our community's use of automated license plate reader technology.

My concern is not simply whether this technology can help investigate crime. We should also understand what information is being collected about residents who are not suspected of any wrongdoing.

Before this program continues or expands, I believe the public deserves clear answers about data retention, data sharing, system access, cybersecurity protections, auditing, and how misuse is prevented.

I am asking the council to publicly review the program and its contract and consider whether the cameras should be removed, the contract should not be renewed, or stronger privacy protections should be adopted.

Public safety and privacy should both be considered when surveillance technology is deployed in our community.

Thank you.

---

# 5. Start a Petition

A petition can demonstrate that concern about a surveillance program extends beyond a single person.

A petition should clearly explain:

- What technology is being challenged
- Which government body controls it
- What residents want changed
- Why the issue matters
- What specific action officials should take

Possible requests include:

- Do not purchase ALPR cameras.
- Pause deployment until a public hearing occurs.
- Do not renew the existing contract.
- Remove existing cameras.
- Require a warrant or other appropriate legal process for certain searches.
- Limit data retention.
- Restrict data sharing.
- Require public reporting and independent audits.

---

# Sample Petition

## Protect Our Community's Privacy

We, the undersigned residents and community members, ask our local government to review its use of automated license plate reader technology.

ALPR systems can provide investigative benefits, but they can also create databases containing information about the movements of large numbers of people who are not suspected of criminal activity.

We ask our elected officials to:

1. Hold a public hearing concerning the community's ALPR program.
2. Publish the current contract and policies governing the technology.
3. Disclose how long ALPR information is retained.
4. Disclose which outside agencies or organizations can access the information.
5. Require meaningful auditing and accountability for system searches.
6. Evaluate whether the existing ALPR contract should be renewed.
7. Consider removing or limiting ALPR technology if adequate privacy protections cannot be established.

Technology affecting an entire community should receive meaningful public oversight.

# 6. Contact Elected Officials

Residents can contact:

- City council members
- County commissioners
- Mayors
- State representatives
- State senators
- Other officials responsible for surveillance policy

Keep communications respectful, specific, and focused on a requested action.

---

# Sample Email to an Elected Official

**Subject: Request for Review of Automated License Plate Readers**

Dear [Official's Name],

I am writing to request a public review of the automated license plate reader program operating in our community.

I would particularly like the government to examine the program's data-retention practices, information-sharing policies, cybersecurity safeguards, auditing procedures, and protections against misuse.

I also ask that officials consider whether the current contract should be renewed and whether additional public oversight or privacy protections are necessary.

Surveillance technology can have legitimate public-safety applications, but technologies capable of collecting information about large numbers of residents should operate with transparency and meaningful safeguards.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

[Name]

# 7. Ask for Specific Policy Changes

Community advocacy does not have to be limited to choosing between keeping every camera and removing every camera.

Residents can also advocate for safeguards.

Possible policies include:

### Shorter Data Retention

Require information unrelated to an investigation or legitimate alert to be deleted after a limited period.

### Restricted Data Sharing

Clearly define which agencies or organizations can access locally collected information.

### Search Requirements

Require users to document a legitimate purpose when searching ALPR databases.

### Audit Logs

Maintain records showing:

- Who performed a search
- When the search occurred
- Why the search occurred

### Regular Audits

Require periodic reviews for improper searches or unauthorized access.

### Public Transparency Reports

Publish information such as:

- Number of cameras
- Number of searches
- Program cost
- Confirmed misuse incidents
- Agencies receiving access

### Public Approval Before Expansion

Require public notice and approval before adding cameras or substantially expanding surveillance capabilities.

### Security Requirements

Require appropriate cybersecurity protections for systems containing surveillance information.

# 8. Build a Community Coalition

Privacy concerns can cross political and ideological boundaries.

Potential participants may include:

- Neighborhood organizations
- Privacy advocates
- Civil liberties organizations
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Journalists
- Community organizations
- Legal organizations
- Students
- Local residents

A larger coalition can divide responsibilities.

For example:

- One person researches contracts.
- Another reviews policies.
- Someone monitors government meetings.
- Someone prepares educational materials.
- Others contact elected officials.

---

# 9. Educate the Community

People cannot participate in decisions about technology they do not understand.

Useful educational materials may explain:

- What ALPR cameras are
- What information they collect
- How databases work
- Data retention
- Data sharing
- Cybersecurity risks
- Potential public-safety benefits
- Privacy concerns
- Oversight options

Avoid exaggerating what the technology can do.

Accurate information makes advocacy more credible.

---

# 10. Advocate Lawfully

Community opposition should focus on lawful civic action.

Do not:

- Damage cameras
- Disable equipment
- Interfere with wiring or power systems
- Trespass to access equipment
- Attempt to hack surveillance systems
- Threaten employees or government officials

Instead use:

- Public records
- Petitions
- Public meetings
- Journalism
- Public education
- Elections
- Legislative advocacy
- Peaceful demonstrations
- Courts and legal organizations when appropriate

Damaging or interfering with surveillance equipment can create legal consequences and distract from legitimate privacy concerns.
