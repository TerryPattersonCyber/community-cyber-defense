# Digital Safety Starter Guide

A beginner-friendly guide to improving everyday digital security and reducing common online risks.

## Why Digital Safety Matters

Phones, computers, email accounts, social media, banking apps, and online services contain large amounts of personal information.

Good digital safety habits can help reduce the risk of:

- Account takeovers
- Identity theft
- Phishing
- Financial fraud
- Unauthorized access
- Data exposure
- Loss of important information

Cybersecurity does not always require advanced technical knowledge. Many of the most effective protections come from simple habits used consistently.

---

## 1. Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Avoid using the same password across multiple accounts.

If one website is breached and your password is exposed, attackers may try the same email and password combination on other services.

### Better password habits

- Use a different password for every important account.
- Make passwords long rather than simply complicated.
- Avoid names, birthdays, phone numbers, and easily guessed information.
- Consider using a reputable password manager.
- Change passwords immediately if you believe an account has been compromised.

### Priority accounts

Start with the accounts that could cause the most damage if compromised:

- Primary email
- Banking and financial accounts
- Apple or Google account
- Social media
- Cloud storage
- Work or school accounts

---

## 2. Turn On Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication, often called MFA or two-factor authentication, adds another layer of protection beyond your password.

Even if someone steals your password, MFA can make it much harder for them to access your account.

Common MFA methods include:

- Authenticator apps
- Security keys
- Push notifications
- Text-message codes
- Email verification codes

Authenticator apps and security keys are generally stronger options than text messages when they are available.

---

## 3. Protect Your Email Account

Your email account is one of your most important digital accounts.

Attackers who gain access to your email may be able to reset passwords for many of your other accounts.

### Protect your email by:

- Using a unique password
- Enabling MFA
- Reviewing recovery email addresses and phone numbers
- Checking recent login activity
- Removing unfamiliar devices
- Being cautious with unexpected password-reset messages

---

## 4. Learn to Recognize Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick someone into revealing information, clicking a malicious link, downloading a harmful file, or sending money.

Phishing can arrive through:

- Email
- Text messages
- Social media
- Phone calls
- Messaging apps
- Fake websites

### Common warning signs

- Urgent threats or pressure
- Requests for passwords or verification codes
- Unexpected attachments
- Suspicious links
- Messages claiming your account will be closed immediately
- Requests for gift cards, cryptocurrency, or unusual payments
- Messages that appear to come from someone you know but feel unusual

When unsure, contact the organization through its official website or app instead of using the link in the message.

---

## 5. Keep Devices Updated

Software updates frequently include security fixes.

Keep the following updated:

- Phone operating system
- Computer operating system
- Web browser
- Apps
- Antivirus or security software
- Smart devices when supported

Enable automatic updates whenever practical.

---

## 6. Lock Your Devices

Use a screen lock on phones, tablets, and computers.

Good options include:

- Strong PIN
- Password
- Fingerprint
- Face authentication

Avoid simple PINs such as:

- 0000
- 1111
- 1234
- Birth years

Set devices to lock automatically after a short period of inactivity.

---

## 7. Review App Permissions

Apps may request access to information such as:

- Location
- Camera
- Microphone
- Contacts
- Photos
- Bluetooth
- Notifications

Review permissions regularly and disable access that is not necessary for the app to function.

Be especially cautious with apps requesting:

- Continuous location access
- Full contact access
- Microphone access
- Camera access
- Accessibility permissions

---

## 8. Be Careful on Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi can be convenient, but users should still take precautions.

When using public networks:

- Avoid sensitive transactions when possible
- Confirm the correct network name
- Prefer websites that use HTTPS
- Keep file sharing disabled
- Avoid automatically connecting to unknown networks
- Use cellular data for highly sensitive activity when practical

---

## 9. Back Up Important Information

Backups can help protect against:

- Device failure
- Theft
- Accidental deletion
- Malware
- Ransomware

Important files may include:

- Photos
- Documents
- School or work files
- Financial records

Consider maintaining more than one copy of important information.

---

## 10. Check Your Accounts Regularly

Watch for unusual activity such as:

- Password-reset emails you did not request
- Login alerts from unfamiliar locations
- New devices connected to your account
- Unexpected purchases
- Changes to recovery information
- Messages sent from your account that you did not send

Act quickly if something appears suspicious.

---

# Quick Digital Safety Checklist

Use this checklist as a starting point:

- [ ] I use unique passwords for important accounts.
- [ ] My primary email has MFA enabled.
- [ ] My financial accounts have MFA enabled.
- [ ] My phone and computer automatically install security updates.
- [ ] My devices use a screen lock.
- [ ] I review suspicious links before clicking them.
- [ ] I know the warning signs of phishing.
- [ ] I review app permissions periodically.
- [ ] I back up important files.
- [ ] I review account activity for suspicious logins.

---

## If You Think an Account Has Been Compromised

Take action as soon as possible.

1. Change the account password.
2. Sign out of other active sessions if the service allows it.
3. Enable or reset multi-factor authentication.
4. Review recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
5. Remove unfamiliar devices or connected applications.
6. Check for unauthorized transactions or messages.
7. Change passwords on other accounts if you reused the compromised password.
8. Contact the service provider if you cannot regain control of the account.
