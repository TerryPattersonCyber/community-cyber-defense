# Privacy Protection Guide

A beginner-friendly guide to understanding what personal information is collected online and taking practical steps to reduce unnecessary exposure.

## Why Digital Privacy Matters

Everyday online activities can generate information about who we are, what we do, where we go, and what interests us.

Websites, apps, devices, advertisers, and online services may collect different types of information depending on how their services work.

Digital privacy is not about completely disappearing from the internet. It is about understanding what information you share, who may have access to it, and making informed decisions about how much information you want to provide.

---

## 1. Understand Your Digital Footprint

Your digital footprint includes information associated with your online activities.

This may include:

- Social media posts
- Photos and videos
- Search activity
- Website visits
- Online purchases
- Location information
- App activity
- Account information
- Device information
- Public records
- Information shared by other people

Some information is intentionally shared, while other information may be collected automatically.

A good privacy habit is periodically asking:

**What information about me is available, who has access to it, and does it need to be available?**

---

## 2. Review Your Privacy Settings

Many online services provide privacy controls that determine how information is collected or shared.

Review privacy settings for:

- Social media accounts
- Google or Apple accounts
- Web browsers
- Shopping accounts
- Email accounts
- Mobile apps
- Smart devices
- Streaming services

Look specifically for settings involving:

- Location
- Personalized advertising
- Activity history
- Search history
- Profile visibility
- Contact syncing
- Data sharing
- Connected applications

Privacy settings can change as services are updated, so review them periodically.

---

## 3. Limit Location Sharing

Location information can reveal sensitive details about your daily activities.

Apps may request location access for legitimate purposes, but not every app needs continuous access.

Review which apps can access your location.

Common options include:

- Never
- Ask next time
- While using the app
- Always

When continuous location access is unnecessary, consider limiting the app to location access only while it is being used.

Also review location-sharing features in:

- Social media
- Messaging apps
- Family tracking services
- Photo applications
- Fitness applications
- Maps
- Connected vehicles

---

## 4. Review App Permissions

Mobile applications can request access to sensitive device features and information.

Examples include:

- Camera
- Microphone
- Contacts
- Photos
- Location
- Bluetooth
- Calendar
- Files
- Notifications

Ask whether the permission makes sense for what the application actually does.

For example, a navigation application may reasonably need location access.

A simple utility application requesting access to your contacts, microphone, and precise location deserves additional scrutiny.

Remove permissions that are unnecessary.

---

## 5. Understand Online Tracking

Websites and applications may use technologies that collect information about how people interact with their services.

Tracking may be used for purposes such as:

- Remembering preferences
- Measuring website performance
- Analytics
- Personalizing content
- Advertising
- Building audience profiles

Cookies are one common tracking technology, but tracking can involve other technologies as well.

When websites provide cookie choices, review the available options instead of automatically accepting everything.

Some sites allow users to limit cookies to those necessary for the service to function.

---

## 6. Reduce Unnecessary Information Sharing

One of the simplest privacy principles is:

**Do not provide information that does not need to be provided.**

Before entering personal information into a website or application, consider why it is being requested.

Sensitive information may include:

- Full date of birth
- Home address
- Phone number
- Social Security number
- Financial information
- Personal email address
- Precise location

Not every service requesting information necessarily needs all of it.

Reducing unnecessary information sharing can reduce the amount of personal data available if an account or service is later compromised.

---

## 7. Be Intentional About Social Media

Social media can reveal more information than people realize.

Posts may reveal:

- Current location
- Workplace
- School
- Family relationships
- Travel plans
- Daily routines
- Birthdays
- Personal interests
- Frequently visited locations

Consider reviewing:

- Who can see your posts
- Who can view your friends or followers
- Whether your profile appears in searches
- Who can tag you
- Whether posts include location information
- What older posts remain publicly visible

Think carefully before publicly posting information that could reveal when your home is unoccupied or where you regularly spend time.

---

## 8. Check Photos Before Sharing

Photos can reveal information beyond the main subject of the picture.

Look for:

- Home addresses
- License plates
- School names
- Employee badges
- Documents
- Computer screens
- Children's identifying information
- Travel information
- Financial information

Photos may also contain location-related information depending on device and application settings.

Review camera and photo-sharing location settings if you do not want location information associated with your photos.

---

## 9. Review Connected Accounts

Many websites allow users to sign in using another account or connect third-party applications.

Over time, you may forget which services have access.

Periodically review connected applications associated with major accounts such as:

- Google
- Apple
- Microsoft
- Facebook
- Other social platforms

Remove applications and services you no longer use or recognize.

---

## 10. Protect Sensitive Documents

Personal documents can contain valuable information.

Examples include:

- Tax documents
- Bank statements
- Identification documents
- Medical documents
- Employment records
- School records
- Insurance information

Avoid leaving sensitive documents publicly accessible through cloud-sharing links or unsecured devices.

Before disposing of physical documents containing sensitive information, use an appropriate secure disposal method.

---

## 11. Review Browser Privacy Settings

Modern browsers provide controls that can help manage privacy.

Depending on the browser, these may include controls for:

- Cookies
- Location access
- Camera access
- Microphone access
- Pop-ups
- Notifications
- Tracking
- Browsing history
- Saved passwords

Periodically review which websites have been granted permissions.

Remove permissions that are no longer necessary.

---

## 12. Understand Data Brokers and People-Search Sites

Some businesses collect information from various sources and create profiles about individuals.

Information may include:

- Names
- Addresses
- Phone numbers
- Age
- Relatives
- Public records
- Previous addresses

Some services provide methods for requesting that certain information be removed or no longer sold.

Because practices and legal rights vary, review the privacy policies and opt-out procedures provided by individual services.

---

# Quick Privacy Checkup

Use this checklist to review your current privacy practices:

- [ ] I have reviewed my social media privacy settings.
- [ ] I know which apps can access my location.
- [ ] I have reviewed camera and microphone permissions.
- [ ] I remove permissions that apps do not need.
- [ ] I think before sharing personal information publicly.
- [ ] I review cookie and tracking options when available.
- [ ] I periodically review connected applications.
- [ ] I check photos for sensitive information before posting.
- [ ] I review browser privacy permissions.
- [ ] I avoid providing unnecessary personal information.
- [ ] I periodically search for publicly available information about myself.
- [ ] I review accounts and applications I no longer use.

---

## A Simple Privacy Principle: Data Minimization

Data minimization means limiting the collection, use, or sharing of personal information to what is actually necessary.

Individuals can apply a similar idea to everyday digital life:

**Share what is necessary, understand why it is needed, and avoid unnecessary exposure of personal information.**

Privacy does not require avoiding technology. It requires making more informed decisions about how technology uses your information.

### Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency

- [CISA – Online Privacy](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/online-privacy)

CISA provides cybersecurity awareness information related to protecting personal information and reducing unnecessary exposure online.
