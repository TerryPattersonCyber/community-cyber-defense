# Scam & Fraud Defense Guide

A beginner-friendly guide to recognizing common scams, protecting personal and financial information, and responding quickly when fraud occurs.

## Why Fraud Awareness Matters

Modern scams can arrive through email, text messages, phone calls, social media, dating platforms, job websites, online marketplaces, and even messages that appear to come from legitimate organizations.

Scammers often rely on social engineering — manipulating people into taking an action rather than directly attacking a computer system.

Their goals may include stealing:

- Money
- Passwords
- Verification codes
- Banking information
- Credit card information
- Social Security numbers
- Personal information
- Access to online accounts

Learning to recognize common warning signs can help stop a scam before information or money is lost.

---

## 1. Recognize the Common Warning Signs

Scams can take many forms, but certain warning signs appear repeatedly.

Be cautious when someone:

- Creates an unusual sense of urgency
- Threatens arrest, account closure, or financial consequences
- Demands immediate payment
- Requests gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers
- Asks for passwords or verification codes
- Sends unexpected links or attachments
- Claims you won something you never entered
- Promises unusually high returns with little risk
- Offers a job that requires you to pay first
- Sends a check and asks you to return part of the money
- Pressures you not to speak with family, friends, your bank, or authorities

Urgency is often used to prevent people from slowing down and verifying the situation.

When something feels unusual, stop and verify before acting.

---

## 2. Phishing

Phishing commonly uses fraudulent messages designed to convince someone to reveal information, visit a malicious website, download a file, or send money.

Phishing messages may pretend to come from:

- Banks
- Employers
- Government agencies
- Delivery companies
- Streaming services
- Social media platforms
- Schools
- Friends or family
- Technology companies

### Common phishing warning signs

Watch for:

- Unexpected password-reset requests
- Suspicious links
- Requests to confirm account information
- Unexpected attachments
- Urgent security warnings
- Requests for verification codes
- Sender addresses that do not match the organization
- Messages asking you to log in through an unfamiliar link

### Safer response

Instead of clicking the link:

1. Open the organization's official application or website yourself.
2. Sign in normally.
3. Check whether the alert appears inside your account.
4. Contact the organization using verified contact information if necessary.

---

## 3. Smishing

Smishing is phishing delivered through text messages or other messaging services.

A message might claim:

- A package cannot be delivered
- Your bank detected fraud
- A toll payment is overdue
- Your account has been locked
- You won a prize
- You qualify for a refund
- Someone needs emergency assistance

Do not assume a message is legitimate simply because it includes your name or other personal information.

Avoid responding to suspicious messages or opening unfamiliar links.

---

## 4. Impersonation Scams

Scammers may pretend to represent someone you trust or recognize.

They may impersonate:

- Government agencies
- Police or sheriff's departments
- Courts
- Banks
- Technology companies
- Utility companies
- Employers
- Family members
- Friends
- Customer support representatives

Caller ID, email addresses, logos, profile pictures, and websites can sometimes be manipulated or copied.

Do not rely only on how professional a message appears.

If someone requests money or sensitive information, independently contact the person or organization using contact information you already know or obtain from an official source.

---

## 5. Verification Code Scams

Verification codes are designed to prove that you control an account or device.

A scammer may attempt to log in to your account and then convince you to provide the verification code sent to your phone or email.

Never give an unexpected authentication or verification code to another person.

A legitimate organization generally does not need you to read back a security code that was sent specifically for your login.

If you receive an unexpected verification code, someone may be attempting to access your account.

Review the account and change your password if necessary.

---

## 6. Job and Employment Scams

Job seekers can be targeted through:

- Job boards
- Social media
- Email
- Text messages
- Messaging applications
- Fake company websites

Warning signs may include:

- Extremely high pay for simple work
- An immediate job offer without a meaningful interview
- Requests to pay for training or equipment
- Requests to deposit a check and send money elsewhere
- Recruiters using personal email addresses instead of company accounts
- Requests for sensitive information before the employer has been verified
- Pressure to communicate only through messaging applications

### Before accepting a job

Verify:

- The company's official website
- Whether the job appears on the company's official careers page
- The recruiter's identity
- The recruiter's email domain
- Whether the company can be contacted independently

Never send money simply to receive a job.

---

## 7. Fake Check Scams

A scammer may send a check and ask you to:

1. Deposit it.
2. Keep part of the money.
3. Send the remaining money somewhere else.

The money may initially appear in your account.

That does not necessarily mean the check is legitimate.

If the check is later determined to be fraudulent, the deposited funds can be removed while the money you sent to the scammer may already be gone.

Be suspicious whenever someone sends you a check and asks you to return, transfer, or spend part of the money for them.

---

## 8. Romance Scams

Romance scammers build emotional relationships before asking for money or financial assistance.

Common stories may involve:

- Medical emergencies
- Travel expenses
- Military deployment
- Family emergencies
- Business problems
- Investment opportunities
- Cryptocurrency
- Difficulty accessing their own money

Warning signs include:

- The person cannot meet in person
- Their stories frequently change
- They quickly develop intense feelings
- They repeatedly experience financial emergencies
- They ask for gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, or money
- They encourage you to invest through a platform they recommend

Never send money or financial information to an online romantic interest you have not independently verified.

---

## 9. Investment and Cryptocurrency Scams

Investment scams may promise:

- Guaranteed profits
- Extremely high returns
- Little or no risk
- Secret investment strategies
- Exclusive opportunities
- Cryptocurrency profits
- Automated trading systems

Be especially cautious when an investment opportunity comes from:

- An unexpected social media message
- An online romantic interest
- A stranger
- An unknown investment group
- Someone pressuring you to act immediately

Legitimate investments involve risk.

Claims of guaranteed high returns with little or no risk should be treated with extreme caution.

---

## 10. Account Takeover Scams

Account takeover occurs when someone gains unauthorized access to an online account.

Targets may include:

- Email
- Banking
- Social media
- Shopping accounts
- Payment applications
- Cloud storage
- Payroll accounts

Attackers may obtain access through:

- Stolen passwords
- Reused passwords
- Phishing
- Fake websites
- Social engineering
- Stolen verification codes

### Reduce your risk

- Use unique passwords.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Never share verification codes.
- Review login alerts.
- Remove unfamiliar devices.
- Keep recovery information current.
- Avoid logging in through unexpected links.

---

## 11. Payment Scams

Scammers often prefer payment methods that can be difficult to reverse or trace.

Be especially cautious when someone unexpectedly demands payment through:

- Gift cards
- Cryptocurrency
- Wire transfers
- Cash
- Payment applications

No legitimate government agency will require you to buy gift cards to resolve a legal problem, pay taxes, or avoid arrest.

Never provide gift card numbers or PINs to someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

---

## 12. AI, Voice Cloning, and Deepfake Scams

Advances in artificial intelligence can make impersonation attempts more convincing.

Technology can be used to imitate:

- Voices
- Images
- Videos
- Writing styles

A message that sounds or looks like someone you know should not automatically be trusted when money or sensitive information is involved.

If a family member, friend, coworker, or executive appears to make an unusual financial request:

1. Stop.
2. Contact that person through another trusted method.
3. Ask a question only the real person would likely know.
4. Verify the request before sending money or information.

Families may also consider creating a private verification word or phrase for emergencies.

---

# Pause, Verify, Protect

When confronted with an unexpected request, remember:

## PAUSE

Do not let urgency force you into an immediate decision.

## VERIFY

Contact the person or organization independently.

Do not use the phone number, email address, or link supplied in the suspicious message.

## PROTECT

Do not provide money, passwords, authentication codes, or sensitive information until the request has been verified.

---

# What To Do If You Have Been Scammed

Act quickly.

## If You Sent Money

1. Contact your bank, card issuer, payment application, wire service, or other payment provider immediately.
2. Tell them the transaction resulted from fraud.
3. Ask whether the payment can be stopped or reversed.
4. Secure the financial account involved.
5. Continue monitoring transactions.

Recovery is not guaranteed, but contacting the payment provider quickly may improve your options.

## If You Shared a Password

1. Change the password immediately.
2. Use a new password that has not been used elsewhere.
3. Enable multi-factor authentication.
4. Sign out of other active sessions.
5. Review connected devices.
6. Check recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
7. Change the password on other accounts if you reused it.

## If You Shared Personal Information

Depending on what information was exposed:

1. Visit IdentityTheft.gov.
2. Follow the recommended recovery steps.
3. Monitor financial accounts.
4. Review credit reports when appropriate.
5. Consider fraud alerts or credit freezes when appropriate.
6. Keep records of suspicious activity and reports.

## If Someone Accessed Your Computer

1. Disconnect from suspicious remote-access sessions.
2. Remove remote-access software you did not intentionally install or no longer need.
3. Update your security software.
4. Scan the device for malicious software.
5. Change important passwords from a trusted device.
6. Monitor accounts for unauthorized activity.

# Fraud Prevention Checklist

- [ ] I stop and verify unexpected financial requests.
- [ ] I do not share verification codes.
- [ ] I use unique passwords for important accounts.
- [ ] I use multi-factor authentication.
- [ ] I verify recruiters and job opportunities independently.
- [ ] I do not pay money to receive a job.
- [ ] I avoid sending money to online romantic interests I have not verified.
- [ ] I independently contact organizations instead of trusting unexpected links.
- [ ] I am cautious when payment is demanded through gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers.
- [ ] I review financial accounts for unauthorized activity.
- [ ] I know where to report suspected fraud.

# Reporting Fraud

Reporting scams can help authorities identify patterns and investigate fraudulent activity.

## Federal Trade Commission

[ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)

Report scams, fraud, and deceptive business practices to the Federal Trade Commission.

## FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center

[IC3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/)

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center accepts reports involving cyber-enabled crime and internet fraud.

## Identity Theft

[IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/)

The Federal Trade Commission's identity theft resource provides recovery guidance and personalized recovery plans.

If someone is in immediate physical danger, contact emergency services.
